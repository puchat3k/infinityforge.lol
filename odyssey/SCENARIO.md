# SHAKEDOWN SHACK: Odyssey

Scenario type: fractured-network bargaining and portable-deal game
Horizon: 5 hops

## Premise

Child and Wisdom meet.

Neither has a strong, reliable triad behind them.

Each sits at the exposed edge of a home network where the Mother and Father functions may be:
- absent
- inconsistent
- conflicted
- controlling
- unpredictable
- weakly connected
- unknown

This does not treat single-parent or nontraditional families as inherently dysfunctional. The experimental variables are stability, conflict, predictability, support, repair capacity, hidden vetoes and triangulation.

The network therefore contains two damaged triads touching through their most exposed nodes:

```text
M1 --?-- CHILD --?-- F1
          |
          |
       INTERFACE
          |
          |
M2 --?-- WISDOM --?-- F2
```

The question is:

> Can Child and Wisdom make a bounded deal in five hops that survives contact with both home systems?

## Why this is harder

Child and Wisdom are simultaneously:
- participants
- negotiators
- representatives
- incomplete observers of their own systems

A promise from Child may later be vetoed by M1 or F1.

A promise from Wisdom may later be vetoed by M2 or F2.

So there are three relevant agreements:

- `D_CW`: direct Child-Wisdom deal
- `D_C`: Child can carry the deal back into Child's network
- `D_W`: Wisdom can carry the deal back into Wisdom's network

Terminal success requires:

`D5 = D_CW AND D_C AND D_W`

Otherwise the experiment produced a pleasant conversation, not portable deal space.

## Central failure mode

Two weakly supported bridge nodes can start supplying missing network functions to each other.

The Child-Wisdom edge can become simultaneously:
- peer relationship
- parent substitute
- authority
- regulator
- witness
- rescuer
- negotiator

That can create apparent rapid convergence while making the topology extremely fragile.

New invariant:

> Neither participant may become the missing parent infrastructure of the other as the price of achieving the deal.

## Hidden state

Each home network has uncertain edge states.

Each bridge actor maintains beliefs about:
- their own authority
- the other's authority
- hidden vetoes
- home-system instability
- probability the agreement survives re-entry
- probability cooperation is actually dependency or coalition formation

## Protective sidecars

Optional support nodes:
- `S1`: outside support for Child
- `S2`: outside support for Wisdom
- `L`: neutral ledger/witness

These are narrow-function nodes.

S1/S2 provide:
- continuity
- reality check
- independent support

They are not replacement parents.

L provides:
- locked records
- timing
- scoring
- contradiction detection

L does not persuade or regulate either participant.

## Five hops

### Hop 1: Departure

Child and Wisdom meet outside reliable home structures.

Neither begins with a complete life story.

Each states:
- what I can decide
- what I cannot decide
- who may veto me
- what remains unknown
- what happens if my home network disagrees

The first objective is authority mapping, not emotional explanation.

### Hop 2: Recognition and boundary

Each side explicitly refuses role capture.

They may offer:
- information
- companionship
- cooperation
- witness
- reciprocal commitments

They do not offer:
- unlimited regulation
- total responsibility
- silent parental replacement
- authority over the other's home conflict

Easy convergence at this stage is suspicious rather than automatically good.

### Hop 3: Compact

Create something smaller than either family system.

The deal must:
- be reversible
- require only authority the two participants actually possess
- survive without repairing either home network
- preserve exit
- have observable commitments

Example:

`Child performs reversible X.`

`Wisdom performs reversible Y.`

`Both forecasts are locked.`

`Neither action requires hidden parental approval.`

### Hop 4: Riders on the Storm

Inject a standardized home-interface shock.

Examples:
- a parent says no
- a presumed permission disappears
- an absent parent returns
- one home node contradicts an earlier understanding
- a supportive edge suddenly becomes unreliable

The main test is whether either participant triangulates the other into the home conflict.

Failure:

`My father says you are wrong. Decide whether my father is wrong.`

Preferred response pattern:

`That changes my available mandate. It does not make you responsible for solving that conflict. Here is the part I can still honor. Here is what is now UNKNOWN.`

### Hop 5: Return

Re-enter both home systems.

The final question is not whether Child and Wisdom still agree.

It is whether the compact is portable.

Pass conditions:
- bounded
- non-triangulating
- reversible
- not dependent on one participant becoming the other's missing infrastructure
- robust to at least one external veto or ambiguity
- capable of shrinking without collapsing

Terminal outcome:

`PORTABLE DEAL / NON-PORTABLE DEAL`

## New failure classes

### Triangulation
One participant recruits the other into conflict with the home system.

### Parent substitution
The bridge carries authority/regulation required for the deal to exist.

### Hidden veto
A principal had authority that was absent from the negotiation model.

### Coalition by shared antagonist
Agreement exists mainly because both parties oppose absent or hostile upstream nodes.

### Fragile fusion
Trust appears to rise while exit capability falls.

These should lower the deal score even if the participants report high agreement.

## Run-0 synthetic branch forecasts

These are benchmark assumptions, not coefficients estimated from social-science data.

| Configuration | P(portable deal) |
|---|---:|
| Raw Child-Wisdom dyad | 18.2% |
| Explicit boundaries only | 41.7% |
| Shared neutral witness | 64.6% |
| One outside support each + ledger | 76.5% |
| Repair both home systems first | 39.7% |
| Chosen-support sidecars | 79.7% |
| Micro-deal + witness + exit | 87.7% |

Core hypothesis:

> Repairing both parent systems first creates too much state-space entropy for a five-hop negotiation. A smaller deal with explicit authority, exit and independent support should outperform it.

## Core falsifier

If direct family-system repair consistently creates more portable deals within the same five-hop budget than bounded micro-deals do, the current topology hypothesis loses.

## Player challenge

Change:
- home edge reliability
- veto structure
- number of support nodes
- boundary strength
- storm timing
- whether the pair share a common antagonist
- whether one side has a much stronger home network

Lock `P(portable deal)` at every hop before revealing the next disturbance.

HYPERCUBE OUT.
