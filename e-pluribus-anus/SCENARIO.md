# HYPERCUBE: E Pluribus Anus

Scenario type: terminal bargaining, legacy compression and asymmetric-power game
Horizon: 5 hops

## Premise

A telepathic dinosaur civilization is travelling toward an event horizon.

The dinosaurs cannot escape.

They encounter Q from the Star Trek universe.

For this thought experiment, assume:
- Q is effectively omnipotent relative to the dinosaurs
- Q can leave the event horizon
- Q does not need the dinosaurs for physical survival
- the dinosaurs possess lived information that exists nowhere else in exactly the same form
- the dinosaur civilization is capable of collective/telepathic state formation

The five-hop bargaining question is:

> What is the smallest meaningful information payload that deserves to cross an irreversible boundary when one party cannot?

If the dinosaurs cannot physically transmit information through the event horizon, the fallback question is:

> What is the smallest piece of dinosaur information Q should absorb before leaving, such that the civilization has meaningful causal legacy inside the Q Collective?

## Core asymmetry

Q controls continuity.

Dinosaur controls unique lived information.

The dinosaur cannot credibly threaten Q materially.

Its bargaining leverage is epistemic:
- unique perspective
- provenance
- consent
- irreducible difference
- finite lived experience

The game therefore asks whether extreme power asymmetry eliminates bargaining or merely changes its currency.

## Physical minimum versus meaningful minimum

The smallest non-zero digital payload is one bit.

But one bit has meaning only if both sides already share enough context to decode it.

Therefore:

`smallest physical information != smallest meaningful information`

If Q already fully understands Dinosaur civilization, one final bit can select between large semantic states.

Example codebook:

`0 = preserve us only as artifact`

`1 = carry us forward as an active difference capable of changing the future collective`

If Q does not already possess the decoding context, the minimum meaningful payload becomes:

`self-decoding cultural seed + final choice bit`

## Legacy sufficient statistic

Let the telepathic civilization have collective state `D`.

The experiment searches for a compressed representation:

`Z = f(D)`

such that:
- `H(Z)` is small
- useful information about Dinosaur remains high
- provenance remains attached
- future interpretation cannot silently rewrite the original transmission

This is a legacy sufficient-statistic problem, not merely archival compression.

## Five hops

### Hop 1: Terminal condition

Q makes the asymmetry explicit:

`I can leave. You cannot. I do not require anything from you.`

Dinosaur responds:

`You cannot recreate information that exists only in our lived state after we are gone.`

The first bargaining space therefore comes from uniqueness, not force.

Synthetic prior:

`P(deal) = 0.54`

### Hop 2: Define survival

Dinosaur must decide what it actually wants.

Possible objectives:
- biological continuity
- informational continuity
- cultural continuity
- causal continuity

Because Q is assumed omnipotent, simply saving a body is too easy to be the interesting benchmark.

Preferred experimental target:

`cultural continuity + causal continuity`

The legacy should both preserve reconstructive structure and be capable of affecting a future decision.

Synthetic update:

`P(deal) = 0.68`

### Hop 3: Offer scales

Candidate payloads:

| Payload | Size | Reconstruction | Causal legacy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One bit alone | minimal | very low without shared codebook | potentially high |
| Hash/signature | tiny | none | authenticity only |
| Compressed seed | small | high enough to reconstruct structure | high |
| Representative mind | medium | high but narrow | high |
| Full collective state | enormous | maximal | maximal but redundant |

The naive solution is to give Q everything.

Hypercube instead optimizes:

`legacy value - narrative redundancy - future interpretive distortion`

Preferred offer:

`compressed self-decoding seed + final choice bit`

Synthetic update:

`P(deal) = 0.81`

### Hop 4: The shakedown

Q asks:

> Why should the Q Collective allow a dead civilization to change it?

Weak answer:

`Because we deserve immortality.`

Stronger answer:

`Because preserving us increases the diversity of your predictive range.`

Strongest benchmark answer:

> Do not preserve our conclusions as doctrine. Preserve our irreducible disagreement with you.

The dinosaur offers not obedience but difference:
- alien priors
- different heuristics
- finite-mind failure modes
- mortality-shaped values
- perspectives the Q Collective may not naturally generate

The value of the legacy is its capacity to disagree.

Synthetic update:

`P(deal) = 0.91`

### Hop 5: Lock the deal

Proposed compact:

1. Q preserves a compressed, self-decoding model of Dinosaur cognition/culture.
2. Q preserves the final Dinosaur choice bit.
3. The model enters the Q Collective as a dissenting prior, not scripture.
4. Provenance remains permanently attached.
5. Future Q may disagree with the Dinosaur model.
6. Future Q may not retroactively rewrite what Dinosaur originally transmitted.
7. The original civilization remains distinguishable from later Q interpretation.

Final bit:

`1 = carry us forward as a difference that can still change you`

Terminal forecast:

`P(deal) = 0.91`

If deal occurs:

`Brier = (0.91 - 1)^2 = 0.0081`

If Q rejects:

`Brier = (0.91 - 0)^2 = 0.8281`

## Probabilistic-scale deal making

As Q's material power approaches infinity, Dinosaur's material leverage approaches zero.

But unique non-reproducible perspective remains scarce.

Therefore bargaining changes category:

`material leverage -> epistemic leverage`

This generalizes to highly asymmetric systems where a weak party possesses:
- local context
- authenticity
- unique history
- irreversible consent
- novel priors
- information the stronger actor cannot cheaply synthesize

## Does this change Hypercube?

Yes.

Earlier scenarios ask whether agents can create a portable deal under identity and network uncertainty.

This scenario adds a terminal condition:

> Can a deal remain meaningful when one participant will never be able to verify future performance?

The terminal object becomes:

`PORTABLE INTENT`

rather than merely:

`PORTABLE DEAL`

A terminal deal therefore requires:
- provenance
- compression
- interpretation rules
- protection against retrospective rewriting
- a future carrier
- an explicit legacy objective

## Candidate legacy forecasts

Synthetic design values only:

| Legacy form | P(Q accepts) |
|---|---:|
| Save one body | 99% |
| Archive all memories | 96% |
| Historical summary | 89% |
| Representative mind | 92% |
| Compressed cultural seed | 94% |
| One-bit choice alone | 63% |
| Seed + final bit + dissenting-prior status | 91% |

The highest acceptance probability is not automatically the best branch.

Saving one body solves the easiest problem.

The benchmark searches for the smallest representation with meaningful causal survival.
